How to Clear the NDA Exam on Your First Attempt: Complete Roadmap for 10th & 12th Students

By Academic Wing, Dehradun Defence College • Published October 2026 • 8 min read

Cracking the National Defence Academy (NDA) entrance exam on your very first attempt is not a matter of luck — it is the result of disciplined, structured execution. Over 4 to 5 lakh aspirants appear for the UPSC NDA examination twice every year, but only around 8,000 to 10,000 clear the written cutoff. Here is the exact blueprint followed by cadets at Dehradun Defence College.

1. Decode the Exam Pattern & Marking Scheme

The NDA written examination consists of two objective papers conducted on the same day:

  • Paper 1: Mathematics (300 Marks, 120 Questions, 2.5 Hours): Requires 25% minimum sectional qualifying cutoff. Penalty of 0.83 marks for wrong answers.
  • Paper 2: General Ability Test / GAT (600 Marks, 150 Questions, 2.5 Hours): Divided into Part A (English - 200 Marks) and Part B (General Knowledge, Science, Social Sciences - 400 Marks). Penalty of 1.33 marks for wrong answers.

2. Master Mathematics Through Concept + Speed Drills

Mathematics is the filter paper. If you fail to clear the sectional cutoff in Paper 1, your Paper 2 is not even evaluated.

Focus heavily on high-weightage chapters: Trigonometry, Matrices & Determinants, Vector Algebra & 3D Geometry, Statistics & Probability, and Calculus. At Dehradun Defence College, we train students to utilize elimination methods and shortcut substitution techniques to solve questions within 45 to 60 seconds.

Director’s Golden Rule: “Never attempt a question by guessing. With negative marking, every 3 wrong answers eliminate the score of one correct answer. Target 60 to 70 high-accuracy questions in Mathematics rather than guessing 100.”

3. Dominate GAT English & General Science

English accounts for 200 marks in GAT and is the highest ROI subject on the entire paper. Build a daily habit of reading editorial columns, learning 10 new vocabulary idioms, and practicing spot-the-error questions.

For General Science, NCERT textbooks of Class 9th and 10th form the foundation. Focus on Optics, Laws of Motion, Electricity, Chemical Reactions, and Human Biology.

4. Early Foundation: The 2-Year Integrated Advantage

Students who begin preparation immediately after the 10th board exams in our 2-Year Foundation Batch experience a massive tactical advantage. They align their school board syllabus with NDA modules seamlessly, leaving no last-minute panic or gap years.

5. Begin SSB Preparation from Day 1

Do not wait for written results before preparing for the SSB interview. Officer-Like Qualities (OLQs) cannot be crammed in 14 days — they must be lived. Practice daily physical exercises (running, pushups, pullups), engage in daily group discussions on current geopolitical issues, and practice clear, concise spoken English.
